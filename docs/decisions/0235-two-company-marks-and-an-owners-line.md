# Hopper's and Bleacher Report's own marks beside their names, with an owners line

## Context and problem statement

No page showed a company's logo; Hopper, Bleacher Report and the rest were names only. LinkedIn shows a logo beside every job, so recruiters are used to them, but a logo adds recognition and no new fact, and a row of logos reads as clients or endorsements. Should company marks show, which, and in what form?

## Decision drivers

- The pictures the site shows are Brad's own; a company's mark is the company's.
- A former employer's name is safe ground; the mark is the part trademark guides flag, so the safe form is small, beside the name, with a line that the marks belong to their owners. This is general information, not legal advice.
- Never redraw a mark or change its colors.

## Considered options

1. Two small marks: Hopper's on the band and Bleacher Report's on its featured card (recommended)
2. None: names only
3. A mark on every timeline row

## Decision outcome

Chosen option 1, on Brad's card in the pictures study's first round (2026-11-16), with the files and the line's words on spec 039's Gate 1 cards the same day.

- Hopper's mark sits beside the band's eyebrow, 30 px tall: the white horizontal logo, the running bunny and "hopper", from Hopper's press kit at https://media.hopper.com/press-kit, taken 2026-11-16. The kit also offers the logo as a coral vector; the white logo, which suits the band's dark ground, is a 1190 × 320 PNG, resized to 223 × 60 for 2x and stripped of metadata.
- Bleacher Report's mark sits beside its featured card's kicker and title, 40 px tall, and above them on a phone: the header mark drawn on https://bleacherreport.com/, taken 2026-11-16, white "B/R" on its #1a1a1a tile, the colors B/R's light scheme draws it in, as an SVG, in both themes. B/R publishes no one-color file, and its brand page now redirects to its home page.
- Each has empty alt text, since its name sits beside it. No other company's mark shows anywhere: never Capital One's, the partners', Bottle Rocket's clients', Harley-Davidson's or Infosys's, and never in the timeline.
- The home and role pages' footer adds, on its own line: "Company names and marks belong to their owners."
- Hopper's terms list its bunny images among its trademarks, and B/R's terms say its logos are its trademarks; neither names rules for color or size. The site uses each mark only beside the company's name, for the work Brad did there, and changes neither.

### Consequences

- Good, because a recruiter recognizes the two employers that matter most, as LinkedIn shows them.
- Bad, because Hopper's file includes its wordmark, so "hopper" shows beside an eyebrow that also says Hopper.
- Bad, because B/R's dark tile barely stands out on the dark theme's card; its white letters still read.

### Confirmation

Spec 039's tests check each mark's file, size, place and alt text, that no other image of a company shows on any page, and the footer line on the home and each role page.
