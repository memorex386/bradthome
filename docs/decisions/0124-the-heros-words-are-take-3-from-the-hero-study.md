# The hero's words are take 3 from the hero study

## Context and problem statement

Slice 3.2 builds the redesigned home page's hero: three words that land one at a time, then two short lines ([plan](../plan.md)). The design canvas's hero study drew three sets of words for the same motion. Which words does the hero use?

## Decision drivers

- A recruiter should read the roles Brad is hiring for, and what Brad builds, in the first screen.
- The words should match the three role links slice 3.6 adds: mobile, full-stack and applied AI.
- No self-ratings, and no claim the page below can't back up.

## Considered options

The options are listed in the builder's ranked order.

1. Take 3: "Mobile." "Full-stack." "Applied AI." with "I build Android, iOS and web products end to end." and "Fast with AI agents, careful with production." (recommended)
2. Take 2: the same three words, with "Hands-on, from architecture to release." and "Fast with agentic workflows, careful with production."
3. Take 1: "Mobile." "Full stack." "AI." with Brad's first two lines

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: "Take 3". It names the platforms recruiters search for, and says Brad owns the whole product. It also says "AI agents" in words every reader knows, and "careful with production" is a promise the Hopper stories below keep.

- The words are "Mobile.", "Full-stack." and "Applied AI.", one per line, in the hero's heading.
- The lines are "I build Android, iOS and web products end to end." and "Fast with AI agents, careful with production."
- The eyebrow above them reads "Senior mobile & full-stack engineer · Dallas–Fort Worth", as Home v2 draws it. From slice 3.6, each role page can swap the role's part.

### Consequences

- Good, because the words, the role links and the résumé PDFs share one vocabulary.
- Bad, because "agentic" drops out of the hero, so the AI card and the résumé carry that keyword.

### Confirmation

Spec 010's tests read the hero's heading and lines word for word.
