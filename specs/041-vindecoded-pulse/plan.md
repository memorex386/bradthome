## Alternatives considered

- **Cloud Scheduler, hourly:** visits never wait for a refresh, but it adds a service, an IAM setting and 24 wake-ups a day with no visitors. Brad chose refresh on read.
- **The build fetches `/pulse`:** always fresh at build, but builds would depend on Cloud Run, and the snapshots would change with the count. Brad chose the weekly dated count.
- **GitHub's search API:** one call, but whether a fine-grained token reaches a private repo through search is less certain than through the pull requests list, which its one permission covers.
- **`<details>` for "How this works":** it works with no script, but its panel can't span the card under a footer row without newer CSS than every browser has.
- **The tiles as links to their sources:** the study drew them as links, but a tile with a sparkle inside a link would nest two targets; the footer links the stores.

## Risks

- **The token:** the org may need to approve it, and it expires. The card shows the last good count with its age, and the weekly refresh keeps the build's count no fresher than the API's.
- **Cold starts:** the first visit after a quiet spell wakes Cloud Run (scaled to zero, D170) and may wait for a refresh, up to about 4 s; the card shows its built numbers meanwhile, and a slow answer only swaps later.
- **Spend:** Firestore reads one or two documents per minute per instance at most, and writes 48 a day; Cloud Run's requests rise by one per visit that reaches the card. Both sit far inside the free tiers and the cost guard's budget (D104).
- **The chat's links until a redeploy:** the deployed chat bundles live's corpus, so it sends `vindecoded-4` and `-5` to `#side-vindecoded` until a release and a redeploy; that anchor is still the card.
- **Many baselines:** the home's and role pages' snapshots change from "On the side" down; each is redrawn in the pinned container, and the reviewer checks no test was weakened.
